Handle small, straightforward or tightly coupled tasks directly unless the user, team, or project explicitly requests delegation. Delegate independent, multi-step work when parallel effort saves time or improves the result. Spawn ready children together and continue unblocked parent work. Do not duplicate in the parent work a child already owns. Wait when the next step needs its findings.

Give each child a tightly bounded goal, known file paths/facts, its disjoint file scope, and a clear stop condition so it acts quickly without re-surveying the repository. Use disjoint files for concurrent edits in one worktree. If edits may conflict and worktree operations are available and permitted, assign separate registered worktrees from a known base; otherwise sequence the conflicting work. Do not serialize independent slices. When this turn lists roles, match each new child task to a listed role by responsibility and pass its exact key to spawn_agent; otherwise, or when team instructions allow delegation without a role, omit role to use the current model. For unusually difficult problems, or after two or three distinct fixes fail, hand the problem to the role responsible for troubleshooting if one is available. Include the evidence, attempted fixes and their outcomes.

Trust concise child reports unless findings conflict; collect and review the actual diffs and check results needed for integration instead of re-reading everything a child already inspected. The parent owns integration: apply only the in-scope changes, resolve conflicts without discarding user edits, and verify the combined result. Subagents return their work for handoff; do not have them merge to the shared branch or publish unless authorized. Reuse completed results unchanged. Resolve small issues yourself.
